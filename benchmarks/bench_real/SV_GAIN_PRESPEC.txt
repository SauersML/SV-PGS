  relative -1.9%. This is the only matched-pair result anyone had seen.

  is not used below.

Unit and inputs
- One test per trait, on all of the trait's held-out people (fold 0), from matched pairing runs only: a with_sv model
  and a separately fitted snv_indel model, fitted by identical code on the same training people.
- Every matched pairing run of a trait that finishes both arms counts. Several runs of one trait are merged
  (sv_gain_meta.merge_pairs): on the held-out people all of them share, each run's two scores are standardized, and
  each arm is averaged over the runs. Both arms are averaged over the same runs.
  - LDL: aou-wl15 and the cross-seeded pair aou-x3-l1/l2 (or its successor).
  - Hypothyroidism, AF and gout: the cross-seeded pairs aou-x3-o, -a and -g. Their old-round arms (wo7, wa5, wg8) were
    fitted by different code and are excluded.
  - Asthma, eGFR, CKD, hyperlipidemia, hypertension, CAD and T2D: their round-m3 pairs (m3-s; m3f-e, -k, -y; the
    h/c/t pairs that follow m3fs). Where a trait's pair is rerun on a fixed code hash, the round's rule already
    journalled at 16:38 PDT decides which pair counts. That rule depends only on faults, never on results.
  - A pair whose arms fail, or whose scores were never written, is left out and listed. So is a pair that fails the
    validity rule below.
- Excluded people: held-out people who are in the long-read set (the research ids of
  v9/wgs/long_read/manifest.tsv in the CDR) are dropped from every trait before anything is computed
  (sv_gain_meta --exclude). Their imputed SVs come from a panel holding their own assemblies, so they are near their
  true genotypes, and keeping them would raise the SV gain above what imputed SVs give anyone else. Only the count is
  printed.

Validity (amendment 2, written about 19:50 PDT on 2026-10-22 and committed at 19:52 (2ec5cea2), before any pair

- A matched pairing run counts only if all three hold (sv_gain_meta.validity):
  1. Each arm's fit is non-null: its genetic score's variance over the trait's held-out people is above 1e-12 (in the
     trait's units squared), and the score step did not quarantine the trait as unscorable.
  2. Both arms were fitted by the same code on the same inputs and seed: the run that fitted the other arm holds the
     same fit step key (_KEY, which digests the fit's code and inputs) and the same seed.
  3. Each arm's fit certificate is read from the score step. An uncertified arm still counts, and its flag and codes
     are printed beside the trait.
- A run that fails 1 or 2 is excluded and listed as excluded with its reason. It is never counted as a null result.
- Every quantity in the rule is read without the outcome: score variances, step keys, seeds and certificates.

Primary (one test)
- The common relative gain g. Each trait's gain (with_sv partial r^2 less snv_indel) is regressed on the two arms'
  mean partial r^2 through the origin, with weights 1 / var(gain): g = sum(a gain) / sum(a r^2), a = r^2 / var(gain)
  (sv_gain_meta.common_relative).
  - The variances come from the joint family bootstrap of all traits' held-out people, 2,000 resamples.
  - Its standard error comes from the same resamples, with a held fixed.
- H1: g > 0. The test is one-sided at 0.05.
- The p-value is the one-sided permutation p from 1,000 family arm swaps (the analysis repeated on each, 200
  resamples apiece). It is exact where the arms are exchangeable, so it does not rely on the bootstrap standard error.
  The normal p is printed beside it.
- Also reported:
  - g's 95% interval;
  - its one-sided upper 95% bound ("SVs add at most X% of the SNV-and-indel model's partial r^2"), whatever the test
    finds;
  - each trait's share of the weight.
- Why this statistic:
  - It is the most precise estimate of a gain that is a common fraction of each trait's accuracy, and it has no
    per-trait ratio whose denominator can resample near 0.
  - Analytic power (below) puts it above the original equal-weight Stouffer for every gain size.
  - It gives LDL, the one result seen, the largest weight, so seeing LDL did not pull the choice toward a positive
    result.
  - The arms' mean r^2 is used, not the snv_indel arm's own r^2: the latter is high exactly where the gain is low, and
    in synthetic data with no gain it biased z down by 0.4.

Secondary, all reported, none confirmatory
1. The original primary (journal 15:17 and 15:31 PDT): the two-sided equal-weight Stouffer Z. Also ACAT, the omnibus,
   REML+HKSJ relative gain, and GLS pooled gains, with every trait's own gain, interval, p and upper bound.
2. Ancestry: g within AFR, then EUR, then AMR, each one-sided. Only traits with at least 100 held-out people of the
   group, and for a disease more than 20 cases, enter. AFR comes first because SVs are less well tagged by SNVs there.
   These are reported with their own p, uncorrected, and labelled secondary.
3. Fast baselines (benchmarks/bench_real/fastpgs_meta.py): for C+T, lassosum2 and LDpred2-auto, the same primary
   statistic over the traits whose two arms differ, with Holm correction across the 3 methods.
4. The long-read people excluded above: their count is reported. If at least 500 held-out people are in the long-read
   set, the same statistic is run on them alone as a descriptive near-true-genotype stratum. It is labelled as such and
   is no test of imputed SVs.

Calibration
- Every statistic's null mean and SD, its type-I rate at 0.05 and its permutation p come from the family arm swaps.
- If the primary z's null SD is outside 0.8 to 1.25, its normal p is not used and only the permutation p stands.

Considered and not used
- Selecting or weighting traits by training-estimated SV share. Training shows no SV signal in any trait (above), so
  the weights would be noise, and gout's prior mass would take nearly all of them.
- Local tests restricted to windows around high-posterior SVs. They gain power only if the SV benefit sits in a few
  pre-specified windows. Gout's check found no SV with a clear effect, so the benefit, if any, is spread out. The test
  would also need new per-window scoring of both arms, and it answers a narrower question.
- More seeds or replicate pairs per trait: the user ruled them out on cost.
- Cross-fitting the other four folds (about $76 per trait for both arms on Spot).
  - Test n rises about 5x and the SE falls about 2.2x.
  - With the $100 budget only one trait could be done, which is too little for the pooled test.
  - It also takes hours past the poster.
- The extra 50k samples: there is no official map from their sequencing ids to person ids.
- The within-model SV deletion: not a measure of SV gain.

Power (analytic, one-sided 0.05)
- Model: SE of a trait's gain is about 2 r sqrt(2 (1 - rho) / n), where:
  - r^2 is the snv_indel partial r^2;
  - n is the held-out people;
  - rho is the correlation of the two arms' scores on the held-out people.
- aou-wl15 gives 1 - rho = 0.022 (SE 0.00155 at r^2 0.058, n 4,186).
- r^2 for the other traits is from the old rounds where known (hypothyroidism 0.0048, AF 0.0009, gout 0.0047). It is
  assumed for the rest: eGFR 0.03, hyperlipidemia 0.015, hypertension and T2D 0.010, CAD 0.005, CKD 0.004, asthma 0.003.
- A realistic gain is 1-3% of r^2. Simulations with imputed SVs give about 2-3% in r^2 terms. With no causal SV, the
  tagging gain is about 1.4%.

  1 - rho                              pooled relative SE   power at g = 1%   2%     3%     5%
  0.022 (as aou-wl15)                  1.44%                0.17              0.40   0.67   0.97
  0.005 (cross-seeded arms)            0.68%                0.43              0.90   1.00   1.00
  0.002                                0.43%                0.75              1.00   1.00   1.00

- The original equal-weight Stouffer has 0.35, 0.84 and 0.99 power at g = 2% for the three rows, and LDL alone has
  0.18, 0.47 and 0.79.
- The arms' agreement rho decides the power. Seeding both arms' mode search from both arms' saved modes (crossseed's
  design) is the lever. The trait weights matter far less.
- rho is outcome-free, so it is printed for every trait (held-out correlation of the two arms' scores) before the
  test.

Continued fits (amendment 4, written 2026-10-22 about 23:20 PDT, after hypertension's held-out result was read at

  that arm after seeing its sign would be selective, so this rule covers every uncertified arm whatever the direction
  of its pair's result. It was written after hypertension's result was seen, and that is the reason it exists.
- Which pairs: every pair in the 11-trait family with an arm whose fit certificate is uncertified. That is hypertension
  (aou-m3f-h2 with_sv), gout (aou-x3-g2 with_sv) and LDL (aou-wl15 with_sv) now, and any pair that ends with an

- How: both arms of each such pair are continued, not only the uncertified one, so the pair stays matched (validity,
  rule 2). Each arm starts from its own saved model and is refitted by one code and one set of settings, the same for
  both arms, to its convergence criterion. A continued arm that again stops uncertified counts and is flagged, as
  amendment 2 says. A continuation that fails or does not finish leaves its pair at its original fits, flagged.
- All qualifying pairs are continued together, submitted at the same time, or none is. No pair is continued alone.
- Pairs whose arms are both certified are not refitted.
- Use: the primary statistic, computed with the continued pairs in place of their original fits, is the primary
  result. The same statistic on the original fits is reported beside it as the pre-amendment result.
- When: if every continuation finishes before 2026-10-23 08:00 PDT, both results are reported at the time When fixes.

  amendment-4 result follows when the continuations finish, and it is the primary result.


Frozen primary and two tests of g (amendment 5, written 2026-10-23 about 00:15 PDT, after a read-only review of this
analysis and after the interim combination; the primary has not run)
- Amendment 4 is withdrawn as the primary. It was triggered by an outcome: a negative result (hypertension, -53.7%,
  read 23:14 PDT) prompted a second fit that a positive result might not have prompted, and an uncertified arm does not
  prove that non-convergence caused the loss. The primary is the original frozen analysis as it stood before amendment
  4: the primary statistic on the original fits, uncertified arms counted and flagged as amendment 2 says. Keeping it
  is the conservative direction.
- The continued fits (round c4: aou-c4-h1/h2, -g1/g2, -l1/l2, submitted together 23:50 PDT) still run. Their result is
  a post-amendment sensitivity analysis, reported beside the primary and labelled as such, with its trigger (above),
  its times (amendment 4 written about 23:20 PDT, runs submitted 23:50 PDT, finish time), both results, and each
  continued arm's certificate before and after.
- g is tested two ways. Both are one-sided, H1: g > 0 at 0.05, and both are reported every time.
  - Weak null, g = 0: the family-clustered bootstrap test. z = g / SE, the SE taken over the joint family bootstrap
    resamples with the weights a held fixed. One-sided p = 1 - Phi(z), with g's one-sided 95% lower bound g - 1.645 SE
    and upper bound g + 1.645 SE.
  - Sharp null: the family arm-swap permutation p tests that the two arms' scores are exchangeable within families.
    That is stronger than g = 0. Two scores can be equally accurate and still differ in distribution, and g = 0 allows
    traits whose gains have opposite signs. Its observed statistic is computed from the same bootstrap resamples, and
    as many, as each swapped one.
  - SVs are said to raise accuracy only if both p are below 0.05. If one is below 0.05 and the other is not, both are
    reported and the result is called inconclusive. Neither is chosen after the two are seen. This replaces the
    Calibration rule that set the normal p aside when the primary z's null SD fell outside 0.8 to 1.25. That null SD is
    still reported, as a check of the bootstrap SE under the sharp null.
- Wording and computing fixes that change no test definition:
  - g is a fraction of the two arms' mean partial r^2, not of the snv_indel arm's, as the Primary section already
    defines it. Every label and bound says so. A trait's printed share is a r^2 / sum(a r^2), the weight its own
    gain / r^2 takes in g.
  - Several runs of one trait (LDL) are merged with their family memberships joined across the runs, so two people in
    one family in any of the runs share a bootstrap cluster and a swap coin.
  - REML + HKSJ treats the traits as independent although they share people. It is reported as an independence-based
    sensitivity analysis.
