Figure 3 | Adding SVs changed test AUC little in each architecture; with true SV genotypes the gain's interval lies above zero for 1 of 17 architectures: 'Well-imputed, 3×'. Paired difference in test AUC on the same held-out people between SV-PGS fitted with and without SVs on one 3 Mb chromosome 22 window (60,000 records), with every SV measured by its true genotype (filled) or imputed by Beagle from SNV haplotypes (open). Each simulation's difference is its mean over the training sizes both genotype sources finished, from 5,000 to 40,000 (Figure 4 shows the sizes apart); mean and Student-t 95% interval over 5-10 simulations per architecture, an arrowhead marking an interval that runs off the axis. Same simulated people, causal variants and fits; only the SV genotypes differ. The widest true-genotype interval, 'Length, type, genes', comes from one simulation at 0.051. The baseline architecture has liability h² = 0.1, prevalence 10%, 200 causal variants and 20% causal SVs with SV effects drawn like small-variant effects; each other architecture changes one or more of these settings or how SV effects are drawn: '3×' triples the SV effect SD, 'a few very large' draws SV effects from a Student t with 3 degrees of freedom, 'all one size' gives every causal SV the same effect size with a random sign, 'well-imputed' draws causal SVs only from those imputed with r² ≥ 0.8, and 'common' only from SVs of frequency ≥ 5%.
