Figure 3 | SVs add accuracy when their effects are large and they are measured well; imputation removes most of the gain. Paired difference in test AUC on the same held-out people between SV-PGS fitted with and without SVs on one 3 Mb chromosome 22 window (60,000 records), with every SV measured by its true genotype (filled) or imputed by Beagle from SNV haplotypes (open): mean and Student-t 95% interval over 8-20 replicates, every training size from 5,000 to 40,000 pooled (Figure 4 shows them apart). The true-genotype interval lies above zero for 7 of 17 architectures. Same simulated people, truth and fits; only the SV genotypes differ. The widest true-genotype interval, 20% SVs, 3× effect, comes from one replicate at −0.085. The baseline architecture has liability h² = 0.1, prevalence 10%, 200 causal variants and 20% causal SVs with SV effects drawn like small-variant effects, and every other changes one thing of it: '3×' triples the SV effect SD, 'a few very large' draws SV effects from a Student t with 3 degrees of freedom, 'all one size' gives every causal SV the same effect size with a random sign, 'well-imputed' draws causal SVs only from those imputed with r² ≥ 0.8, and 'common' only from SVs of frequency ≥ 5%.
