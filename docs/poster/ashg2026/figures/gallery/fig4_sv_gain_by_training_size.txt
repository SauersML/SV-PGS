Figure 4 | The gain from adding SVs at each training size from 5,000 to 20,000, for each simulated architecture. Each panel shows the paired difference in test AUC between SV-PGS fitted with and without SVs on one 3 Mb chromosome 22 window, with SVs measured by their true genotypes (filled) or imputed by Beagle (open): the mean over replicates and its Student-t 95% interval. Each panel has its own y-axis scale. A mean is drawn when its training size has at least three replicates. Each replicate uses the same simulated truth and the same test people at every training size. We fitted 0-3 replicates per architecture and genotype source at 40,000 training people, too few to show; Figure 3 pools them with the other sizes. The baseline architecture has liability h² = 0.1, prevalence 10%, 200 causal variants and 20% causal SVs with SV effects drawn like small-variant effects, and every other changes one thing of it: '3×' triples the SV effect SD, 'a few very large' draws SV effects from a Student t with 3 degrees of freedom, 'all one size' gives every causal SV the same effect size with a random sign, 'well-imputed' draws causal SVs only from those imputed with r² ≥ 0.8, and 'common' only from SVs of frequency ≥ 5%.
